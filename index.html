About this map

Purpose. Energy maps usually show either demand (buildings) or supply (the grid), never both. This map puts New York City's building energy use and its in-city generation and transmission assets on one canvas, so the concentration of demand — and how little is generated near it — reads at a glance.

Demand. Buildings over 25,000 ft² must report annual energy use under NYC Local Law 84. This map uses the calendar-year 2024 disclosure (NYC Open Data 5zyy-y8am, rows updated Nov 2025). Buildings were geocoded by tax lot (BBL) using PLUTO and aggregated to the 2020 Neighborhood Tabulation Areas. Cleaning: campus parent/child duplicates (identical totals reported on several rows) are collapsed to one reporting unit, and rows with site EUI outside 5–500 kBtu/ft² are dropped as reporting errors. Site energy is converted at 3.412 kBtu per kWh; natural gas at 29.3 kWh per therm. Points show the 4,000 largest consumers; neighborhood colors include all cleaned records.

Supply. Power plants come from the U.S. EIA Energy Atlas (EIA-860 nameplate capacity by fuel), filtered to the five boroughs. Transmission substations come from the HIFLD electric substations layer (imagery-derived, 2015 vintage; many entries carry no name or voltage). Substations do not include Con Edison's underground area substations.

Caveats. Site energy is energy consumed at the building, not primary energy; district steam and cogeneration blur the electricity share for large campuses. LL84 covers large buildings only (about 60% of the city's floor area), so neighborhood totals are a floor, not the whole picture. Nameplate MW is not generation: most in-city plants are peakers that run a few hundred hours a year.
